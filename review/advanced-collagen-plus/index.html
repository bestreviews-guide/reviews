Does It Work? What the Evidence Says

Collagen itself isn't a new idea, and the research behind it is better than I expected. A 2026 meta-analysis of 19 randomised trials with 1,341 people found that collagen supplements improved skin hydration and reduced wrinkle depth. The trials mostly used 5–15 g a day over 8–12 weeks, which puts this product's 11 g a day squarely in the studied range. That lines up with the company's own FAQ, which says most people report noticing a difference from around 4 weeks.

The joint evidence is thinner. Collagen peptides may support cartilage, but the trials are smaller and less consistent than the skin studies, so I'd treat joint comfort as a possible bonus rather than a promise. And the chondroitin is the weak spot in the formula: joint studies typically use 800–1,200 mg a day, while this scoop gives you 10 mg. At that level it isn't doing the heavy lifting — the collagen is.

One thing I want to be straight about: there is no published trial of this exact product, so we can't say for certain that this blend works the way the individual ingredients did in the studies. What I can say is that the dose is in the right range, the label is honest about what's in it, and the skin evidence for collagen in general is genuinely encouraging.
