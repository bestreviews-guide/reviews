What Does the Evidence Actually Say?

I'll give the formula this much: its two main ingredients aren't made up. The sales page cites HMB research, including a study published in the Journal of Cachexia, Sarcopenia and Muscle in 2020, and HMB has been studied for years as a way to reduce muscle breakdown and support strength, especially in older adults. Dileucine is newer, but the 2,000 mg dose in this product matches the dose used in the research, which is exactly what I want to see — too many supplements use a token sprinkle of a trendy ingredient and hope nobody checks.

But there are limits, and they're important. First, the HMB dose is 1,500 mg a day, which is half what most trials used — so you're not getting the full studied dose of HMB, even though you are getting the studied dose of dileucine. Second, the research is on the individual ingredients, not on this exact tablet or this exact combination, so I can't honestly say the product itself has been proven to build muscle in a clinical trial. And third, no ingredient in this bottle replaces training. Muscle protein synthesis needs a trigger — resistance exercise — plus enough protein in your diet. A tablet can support that process, but it can't do the work on its own, and anyone who tells you otherwise is selling you something.
