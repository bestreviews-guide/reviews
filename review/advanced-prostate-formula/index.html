What Does the Evidence Say?

The ingredients have research behind them; the product itself doesn't — and the most famous ingredient in the bottle failed its biggest test. Saw palmetto is the most trusted natural remedy for prostate health, and it helps block the conversion of testosterone into DHT. But the largest, best-designed trial — a 2011 JAMA study — found saw palmetto no better than placebo for prostate symptoms, even at triple the standard dose. The National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health reaches the same conclusion in plain language.

Beta-sitosterol is the strongest ingredient here. Trials using 60–130 mg a day found it improved urinary flow and reduced residual urine volume in men with BPH — real, measurable effects. But the dose matters, and this label holds 50 mg — just under the trial range. DIM has lab and animal research on hormone pathways, but no large human trial for prostate symptoms, though 150 mg is a sensible dose. Lycopene has observational research linking tomato intake to prostate health, but that's diet, not a tablet. And zinc deficiency is rare in men who eat normally, so extra zinc doesn't shrink a prostate.
