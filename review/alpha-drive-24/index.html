What Does the Evidence Actually Say?

I couldn’t find any published clinical trial of Alpha Drive 24 itself, or of the Endurologix Essence blend as a whole. I also couldn’t find any human studies of Alpha Drive 24. That means there’s no direct evidence that this exact combination, at these unknown doses, does what the marketing suggests.

That leaves the individual ingredients. Some of them have been studied on their own — but a study of one ingredient at a known dose doesn’t prove anything about a blend where that ingredient’s dose is hidden. You can’t borrow a result from a trial and apply it to a product when you don’t know whether the product contains anything like the trial dose.

Of the ingredients on the label, Boron and Tongkat Ali (Eurycoma longifolia) are the two with the strongest basis for testosterone support, and there’s a 2025 review of Eurycoma longifolia that looks at its effects on testosterone. Even so, that’s evidence about Tongkat Ali in general, not about Alpha Drive 24, and it says nothing about how much Tongkat Ali is in each serving here. Zinc and fenugreek are sometimes talked about in this category, but they aren’t in this formula at all, so don’t expect them here.

So my honest read is this: the blend contains some interesting, traditionally used ingredients, but the evidence for this product as sold is weak, and the missing doses make it impossible to judge properly. That’s a big part of why my score sits in the middle rather than near the top.
