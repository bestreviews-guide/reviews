FemiCore Reviews: I Read the Label So You Don't Have To

By Dr. Evans, Independent Supplement Reviewer. I have been getting asked about FemiCore a lot lately, so I sat down with the bottle and the label and did what I always do - read every ingredient, check every dose against the studies, and see whether the price makes sense.

My verdict is 3.6 out of 5. A sensible idea with a hidden label. That is the short version, and the label is where the story sits.

FemiCore Reviews

That is my full review, with the label checked line by line. FemiCore is a women's bladder-health capsule, taken once a day. One bottle holds 30 capsules, a 30-day supply. The idea is cranberry plus five Lactobacillus probiotic strains, aimed at the urinary microbiome.

The label is the heart of it. It names nine ingredients, grouped into a 350 mg herbal blend and a 50 mg probiotic blend. The nine are mimosa pudica seed extract, cranberry extract, bearberry leaf and granular berberine in the herbal blend, with Lactobacillus crispatus, Lactobacillus acidophilus, Lactobacillus plantarum, Lactobacillus gasseri and Lactobacillus casei in the probiotic blend. But not one individual dose is published, and there is no CFU count for the probiotics, so I can tell you what is in it, but I cannot tell you how much of anything you are actually getting.

The price is $79 a bottle if you buy two ($158), $69 a bottle for three ($207), and $49 a bottle for six ($294). The two-bottle pack adds $9.99 shipping in the US; the bigger packs ship free.

The guarantee is the best part of the offer. It runs for 60 days, money back, and it covers opened bottles. ClickBank is the retailer of record and handles the refunds.

Who should buy it: someone who wants a cranberry-and-probiotic approach to urinary health and will judge it on urinary health, not leaks. Who should skip it: anyone buying it mainly to stop leaks - nothing here proves it stops leaks or urgency, there is no trial of the finished formula, and I could not find a single buyer review I could verify. Anyone who is pregnant, nursing, or on medication should ask their own doctor first. This is general information only, not medical advice.

My take: buy it for the cranberry-probiotic idea and the refund window, not for the promise of leak-free living. The idea has real science behind it for urinary health, but the hidden label and the leak claim are what hold the score at 3.6.
