What Does the Evidence Actually Say?

Here's where I have to be straight with you: there's no clinical trial on Goliath XL 10 itself. Everything the product claims rests on studies of its individual ingredients — and those studies mostly used bigger doses, different forms, or animals.

Take ginkgo. The trials that get cited used 240 mg a day; this capsule has 120 mg, half that. A 2024 review went further and found ginkgo had no significant effect on erectile dysfunction at all. Hawthorn looks a little better on paper, but a 2022 review described the evidence as low-quality. Epimedium's star compound, icariin, worked in rats — not the same as working in men.

The pattern repeats down the label. Damiana's support comes from rodents. Muira puama's best study was small and low-quality. Tribulus has a small 2017 meta-analysis behind it, but the trials were few and tiny. Saw palmetto did no better than a placebo in a meta-analysis, and vitamin E trials found no significant effect. None of that makes the capsule useless — a blend of circulation-friendly herbs could help some men a little — but it does mean the headline promises are doing a lot of heavy lifting the research can't support.
