What Does the Evidence Say?

Short version: the ingredients are genuinely studied, the finished blend is not. Ginger is the strongest ingredient here. A systematic review from 2000 found ginger effective for nausea, and later trials back it up for motion sickness and post-operative nausea. It is also used in traditional Chinese medicine for dyspepsia — the exact job this formula assigns it.

Digestive enzymes are the most practical part of the blend: people with low enzyme output — after gallbladder removal, with pancreatic issues, or for other reasons — genuinely benefit from enzyme supplementation. Lion's mane has small human trials for gut and cognitive health, and black pepper's piperine genuinely boosts nutrient absorption. But no trial has tested this 21-ingredient blend as a whole, and no individual herb dose is published. So the fair summary is a broad, sensible gut formula with real ingredients in mostly unknown amounts, not a proven treatment for anything.
