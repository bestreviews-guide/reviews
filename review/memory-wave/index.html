How Does It Work? What the Evidence Says

Gamma is the fastest brainwave band, roughly 30 to 100 Hz, and 40 Hz sits in the middle of it. The idea behind Memory Wave is gamma brainwave entrainment at 40 Hz: listen to a beat at that frequency and your brain activity is supposed to follow along. That is the claim. Now here is where the research underneath it, and the leap from it, need to be kept apart.

Start with the research, because it deserves respect. In 2016, Iaccarino and colleagues at MIT showed that 40 Hz stimulation reduced amyloid in Alzheimer's-model mice (Iaccarino et al., Nature 2016). In 2019, Martorell and colleagues extended it: 40 Hz sound stimulation improved spatial and recognition memory in mice (Martorell et al., Cell 2019). There is also a 2019 meta-analysis by Garcia-Argibay and colleagues on binaural beats (Psychological Research 2019). That is a real, peer-reviewed result about the method in general. It is not a test of this track, and it should not be read as one.

That gap is the whole story of this product. The hook is real science. The leap is the vendor's. The research used different delivery, different doses, and different subjects: the mouse work used an hour a day of lab-delivered light and sound, not a twelve-minute consumer audio file. Nobody has run a controlled trial on this specific file to close that distance, and there is no published trial of Memory Wave itself.

The human trials that followed have been thin. The largest published human trial missed its main efficacy outcome — OVERTURE (76 people, six months, sham-controlled) met its safety and tolerability outcomes but found no separation from sham on its main efficacy measures, the MADCOMS composite, CDR-SB, or ADAS-Cog14. A larger 673-person pivotal trial completed in August 2026 with no published topline results as of late 2026. So the human evidence has not caught up with the mouse work, and it does not support the product on its own.

That does not make Memory Wave a scam. It makes it an honest low-cost experiment with a big unproven step in the middle — and that is exactly how I would treat it.
