Does ProDentim Work? What the Evidence Says

Short version: the strains have papers behind them. The tablet itself doesn't. And the biggest claims — stronger teeth, a whiter smile, gums healed — have never been tested in a trial of ProDentim itself.

The strongest evidence is for L. reuteri. Small randomized trials have found L. reuteri lozenges reduce gum bleeding and plaque in people with gingivitis, usually over 2 to 4 weeks. Inulin, as a prebiotic, is well established for feeding beneficial bacteria, and tricalcium phosphate supplies the calcium and phosphate ions that saliva uses to buffer acid and remineralise early enamel damage — that's basic dental chemistry.

But the gap is real. The L. reuteri trials used specific strains at specific doses in people with diagnosed gingivitis — and ProDentim doesn't disclose its per-strain dose, so you can't check whether it matches. None of those studies tested ProDentim. There's no trial showing a chewable probiotic whitens teeth or rebuilds enamel, because enamel has no living cells and doesn't regrow.
