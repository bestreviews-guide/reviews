What the Evidence Says

I have to split the evidence three ways: the ingredients have research behind them, the finished product doesn’t, and the doses in between are unknown. The strongest ingredient evidence is for pygeum and beta-sitosterol. A 2002 Cochrane review found standardised pygeum “may be a useful treatment option” for BPH symptoms, and a 2000 Cochrane review of beta-sitosterol found improved symptoms and urine flow. Saw palmetto is the outlier: a 2023 Cochrane review of 5,533 men found little to no benefit versus placebo.

But here’s the gap. Those reviews tested single ingredients at known doses — pygeum at 100 mg a day in the trials, for example — while Prosta Peak prints no dose for any ingredient. There is no published, independent clinical trial of Prosta Peak itself, and no supplement has been shown to cure BPH. So what you’re really buying is a capsule whose best ingredients are studied, at doses this label keeps hidden.
