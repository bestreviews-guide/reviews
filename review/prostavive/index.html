Does ProstaVive Work? What the Evidence Says

In short: the plants have papers behind them. The tub does not. The strongest ingredient evidence is for fenugreek and ashwagandha. A 2016 randomized trial found a standardized fenugreek extract improved testosterone levels and libido in healthy men over 12 weeks (Rao et al., 2016). Ashwagandha has a proper randomized trial showing reduced stress and cortisol in chronically stressed adults (Chandrasekhar et al., 2012). Nettle root has small trials for urinary symptoms in men with benign prostate enlargement, using 240 to 459 mg a day — more than the 200 mg in this scoop. Tongkat ali has small trials for testosterone in men with low levels.

Here is the dose check, in numbers. The fenugreek trial used 600 mg a day of a standardized extract; this scoop holds 500 mg of plain fenugreek powder — 83% of the trial amount, and a different form. The ashwagandha trial used 600 mg a day (300 mg twice daily); this scoop holds 250 mg — 42% of the trial dose. So one headline ingredient sits near its trial dose and the other sits under half of it. And none of those studies tested ProstaVive: the fenugreek trial measured testosterone, not prostate size or urine flow, and the circulation theory rests on general blood-flow research, not on a trial of this blend. Judge it for what it is — a mostly transparent powder with reasonable ingredients at mostly real doses — and do not expect a prostate cure, because no powder has ever been shown to shrink a prostate.
