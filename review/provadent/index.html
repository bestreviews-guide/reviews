Does ProVadent Work? What the Evidence Says

Short answer: the ingredients have papers behind them. The tablet doesn’t have one. And the biggest dose on the label is still well below the trial amounts, so keep your expectations modest.

The strongest ingredient evidence is for xylitol. Multiple randomized trials and reviews have found that xylitol can reduce cavity-causing bacteria, but those trials mostly used gum or mints several times a day, totalling 5 to 10 g a day. This serving is 900 mg — just 18% of the 5 g figure — so you can’t assume the trial results carry over at this dose.

Cranberry proanthocyanidins have research for preventing bacteria from sticking to surfaces, mostly in urinary-tract studies rather than dental ones. The BLIS K-12 strain (Streptococcus salivarius K12) has small trials for throat and oral health, and L. reuteri has small trials for gum bleeding and plaque. Those are real studies, but they’re small, and they tested individual strains — not this finished tablet.

Here’s the gap in that story: no published trial of the finished ProVadent product on any dental outcome exists. Judge it for what it is — a chewable with a real xylitol dose, a published enzyme complex, and four probiotic strains — and don’t expect it to do what brushing, flossing and a dentist do.
