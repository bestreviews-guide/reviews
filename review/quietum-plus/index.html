Does It Work? What the Evidence Says

Put plainly: the ingredients have research behind them, the product itself does not, and none of that research was done on hearing. The trials behind these ingredients are about stress, mood, nausea and circulation — not hearing, and not tinnitus. No trial has tested Quietum Plus itself.

Ashwagandha has the strongest trial behind it: a randomised, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial from 2012 gave 64 stressed adults 300 mg of ashwagandha root extract twice a day for 60 days and found stress scores and morning cortisol both fell well beyond placebo. That’s a real result — for stress, not for hearing. Maca has a 2010 systematic review pooling four small trials, which found limited evidence for sexual desire and warned the trials were too few and too small for firm conclusions. Ginger has real evidence for nausea from a 2000 systematic review — but nausea isn’t hearing.

L-arginine genuinely widens blood vessels through nitric oxide, and blood flow to the inner ear matters — but no trial has tested L-arginine for hearing. Zinc, in this bottle at an undisclosed dose, was covered by a 2016 Cochrane review of three trials with 209 participants, which found no evidence that oral zinc improves tinnitus symptoms.
