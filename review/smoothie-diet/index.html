The Evidence: What The Research Actually Says

This is the part of the review I liked most, because the plan leans on real evidence rather than hype. The strongest study the review cites is Astbury et al. 2019, published in Obesity Reviews. It pooled 23 trials with 7,884 adults and found that meal-replacement programs led to about 1.4 kg more weight loss at one year than standard diets - and up to around 6 kg more when people also got extra support. That's a solid, honest result: meal replacements work, and support helps them work better.

The second study is Heymsfield et al. 2003, which pooled 6 trials and found that people using meal replacements lost about 7-8% of their body weight, compared with 3-7% for people on standard low-calorie diets. Again, that's a real difference, and it lines up with the Astbury finding.

But here's the honest catch, and it's an important one: those studies tested meal replacement in general, not this program. There's no trial of The Smoothie Diet itself, no independent review site with an average rating I could find, and no Trustpilot page for it. So the evidence says the approach works. It doesn't prove that this particular set of recipes, at this particular calorie level, will work for you. Don't let anyone blur that line.
