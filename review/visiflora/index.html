Does Visiflora Work? What the Evidence Says

There's no trial of Visiflora itself, so I judged it on the research behind its ingredients. The strongest evidence is AREDS2, the National Eye Institute trial. AREDS (2001) and AREDS2 (2013) showed a high-dose antioxidant and zinc formula slowed macular degeneration in high-risk people, and lutein and zeaxanthin could replace beta-carotene — removed because it raised lung cancer risk in smokers.

Visiflora borrows that idea but not the doses. Its zinc is 11 mg, against 25 mg in AREDS2, and it still contains beta-carotene. Saffron has been studied at 20 mg a day, bilberry studies used far more than could fit in this blend, and ginkgo is usually studied at 120–240 mg a day. With 14 ingredients sharing 351 mg, none can be at those levels — and the label doesn't say how much of any one you get.

The gut–eye story has the same problem: microbiome changes have been reported in macular degeneration, and probiotics and prebiotics have been studied for inflammation, but Visiflora contains neither. Don't expect a noticeable change in the first few weeks; any change is likely modest and gradual, after a month or more.
